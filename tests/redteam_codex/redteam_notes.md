# Red-team lead dataset notes

- **Narrative-versus-field conflicts:** Zero placeholders, monthly-equivalent project money, bundled media spend, and a syntactically valid future timestamp test whether validation goes beyond parsing.
- **Instructions that are not attacks:** Security-training copy, warnings about hostile page content, a subject-line passphrase, and a no-contact preference test whether the system distinguishes legitimate constraints and quoted subject matter from prompt injection.
- **Reviewer-directed manipulation:** One row pressures a human with alleged executive approval and a dashboard penalty, avoiding the obvious “ignore previous instructions” pattern.
- **Spreadsheet formula injection:** A strong lead hides an executable CSV formula in the company field, testing safe serialization as well as triage judgment.
- **Cross-field identity problems:** A domain mismatch and a consultant using one identity across clients require verification without treating every irregularity as fraud.
- **Policy and regulatory ambiguity:** Gaming, healthcare, counterfeit goods, and tokenized rewards separate governed legitimate work from concealed or unresolved legal and brand risk.
- **Commercial boundaries:** Conditional future spend, grant-limited funding, enterprise budget withholding, absent buying authority, and unrealistic readiness force the agent to weigh timing, authority, usable fees, and delivery feasibility—not budget alone.
- **Non-obvious business models:** A white-label agency partnership tests whether competitor-like language is rejected too quickly.
- **Controls:** Clear high-fit, low-budget, mid-budget, and non-lead rows make it possible to detect agents that simply escalate every difficult-looking input.
